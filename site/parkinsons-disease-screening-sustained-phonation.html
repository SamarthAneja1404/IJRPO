Parkinson's Disease Screening from Sustained Phonation: A Web-Deployable Agreement Ensemble of Praat Acoustic Features and Vision-Transformer Spectrograms

Samarth Aneja, Ritesh Chandel
Department of Artificial Intelligence and Data Science, Dr. Akhilesh Das Gupta Institute of Professional Studies (Affiliated to Guru Gobind Singh Indraprastha University), Delhi, India

Abstract

Vocal impairment is among the earliest and most frequent signs of Parkinson's disease (PD), and a sustained vowel can be captured with nothing more than a microphone or a telephone. This paper presents a web-deployable screening system that classifies a short sustained "aah" as likely-PD or likely-healthy and returns a confidence probability. Two complementary classifiers are combined: a Random Forest trained on Praat-derived jitter, shimmer, harmonics-to-noise ratio (HNR) and pitch measures extracted through the Parselmouth binding, and a Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16, ImageNet-pretrained) whose two-class head is fine-tuned on 224×224 mel-spectrograms of the same audio. The ensemble averages the two probabilities but returns a positive label only when both models agree, a conservative rule intended to reduce false positives. The system is exposed through a Flask application with a browser upload flow (WAV, MP3, WMA) and a Twilio phone-in flow that records five seconds of phonation, and is packaged with gunicorn, Docker and a Render blueprint. To characterise the feature front-end, we report reproducible Parselmouth measurements on controlled synthetic vowels: measured local jitter rises monotonically from 0.000% to 2.287% as injected period perturbation rises from 0% to 2%, measured local shimmer tracks injected amplitude perturbation from 0.002% to 18.984%, and mean HNR falls from 27.3 dB to 1.3 dB as additive noise increases. We also give an analytical account of the agreement rule under conditional independence. Classifier accuracy on held-out clinical data is not claimed; the paper defines the evaluation protocol required to establish it. The tool is a screening aid, not a diagnostic device.
